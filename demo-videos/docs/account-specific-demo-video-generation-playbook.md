# Account-Specific Demo Video Playbook

## Purpose

Use this playbook to create a short, account-specific field-enablement or concept-demo video for any company, industry, product, workflow, creator role, or viewer role.

The default deliverable is a 45–60 second horizontal video that:

1. Opens with a relevant operational trigger.
2. Shows why the current situation is difficult.
3. Demonstrates one credible workflow.
4. Keeps an accountable person in control.
5. Ends with one measurable next step.

The video should explain one decision path—not an entire platform or company strategy.

---

## 1. Define the brief

Complete these variables before scripting or sourcing footage.

| Variable | Definition |
|---|---|
| `{PROVIDER_COMPANY}` | Company presenting the solution |
| `{TARGET_COMPANY}` | Account or organization receiving the video |
| `{CREATOR_ROLE}` | Person using the video: PMM, seller, SE, CSM, consultant, product leader |
| `{VIEWER_ROLE}` | Primary audience: executive, technical leader, domain expert, operator, risk owner |
| `{WORKFLOW}` | One workflow being demonstrated |
| `{TRIGGER}` | Event that begins the story |
| `{EVIDENCE}` | Inputs needed to understand the event |
| `{FRICTION}` | Delay, fragmentation, uncertainty, inconsistency, or manual work |
| `{SOLUTION}` | Product or service capability being demonstrated |
| `{HUMAN_APPROVER}` | Person accountable for the decision |
| `{DECISION}` | Action the workflow prepares or supports |
| `{OUTCOME}` | Operational or business improvement |
| `{CTA}` | One requested next step |
| `{DISCLOSURE_STATUS}` | Official, approved concept, internal draft, or personal concept |

### Objective statement

> After watching, `{VIEWER_ROLE}` should understand how `{SOLUTION}` could help `{TARGET_COMPANY}` move from `{TRIGGER}` to `{DECISION}` using stronger `{EVIDENCE}` with clear `{HUMAN_APPROVER}` control—and agree to `{CTA}`.

Choose a workflow that is relevant, urgent, visual, measurable, and supported by credible proof. Prefer a clear sequence such as Detect → investigate → explain → approve → act.

---

## 2. Adapt to the roles

| Role | Emphasize | Recommended CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Product marketer | Use-case relevance, differentiation, outcome | Define a measurable pilot |
| Seller | Account priority, urgency, business value | Select one high-value workflow |
| Solution engineer | Inputs, integration, controls, traceability | Map architecture and data access |
| Customer-success manager | Adoption, governance, expansion | Prioritize an expansion workflow |
| Consultant | Operating model and decision process | Run a discovery workshop |
| Product manager | User friction and workflow behavior | Validate the workflow with users |
| Executive sponsor | Strategic consequence and measurable result | Sponsor a bounded pilot |

Adjust proof for the viewer:

- **Executives:** stakes, control, outcome, CTA
- **Technical leaders:** architecture, boundaries, evaluation, observability
- **Domain experts:** evidence, exceptions, reasoning, review
- **Operators:** usability, handoffs, speed, clarity
- **Risk owners:** uncertainty, approval, provenance, audit trail

---

## 3. Use one narrative structure

| Time | Beat | Message | Visual requirement |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:03 | Trigger | Something important just happened | Three fast, literal impact shots |
| 00:03–00:08 | Stakes | Delay or uncertainty matters | Consequence or operating context |
| 00:08–00:14 | Complexity | Evidence is fragmented | Diverse sources and signals |
| 00:14–00:21 | Solution | Bring inputs into one controlled workflow | Concise product proof |
| 00:21–00:29 | Reasoning | Compare, challenge, or request evidence | Literal comparison and uncertainty |
| 00:29–00:37 | Human control | An accountable expert reviews and approves | Review, challenge, approval |
| 00:37–00:44 | Outcome | Move toward a better decision | Action, resolution, traceability |
| 00:44–00:52 | CTA | Agree on one next step | Dedicated end card |

For a 30-second cut, rewrite the script; do not accelerate the longer version. Use extra time in a 60–90 second cut for one deeper proof point, exception branch, governance control, or pilot measure.

---

## 4. Write the script

### Generic narration template

> `{TRIGGER}` detected.
>
> The evidence is scattered—and every second matters.
>
> The signal could begin anywhere: `{EVIDENCE_SOURCE_1}`, `{EVIDENCE_SOURCE_2}`, `{EVIDENCE_SOURCE_3}`, or `{EVIDENCE_SOURCE_4}`.
>
> `{SOLUTION}` brings every source into one controlled, traceable workflow.
>
> It compares what happened before, surfaces conflicting signals, and builds a concise decision brief.
>
> Not enough confidence? It asks for more evidence instead of guessing.
>
> `{HUMAN_APPROVER}` reviews the reasoning, challenges it, and adds judgment.
>
> Only an approved action moves forward, with every decision traceable.
>
> The result: `{OUTCOME}` and a clear path to `{DECISION}`.
>
> Let’s `{CTA}`.

### Script rules

- Use one idea per sentence.
- Name concrete evidence and actions.
- Describe what the workflow does, not what the technology “is.”
- State uncertainty and human approval when consequential decisions are involved.
- Remove unsupported metrics and generic claims.
- End with one CTA.
- Distinguish proposed, demonstrated, deployed, and measured behavior.

Never present a concept as a deployed customer workflow.

---

## 5. Direct the narration

Generate or record each sentence separately so timing, pronunciation, pace, pitch, emphasis, and retakes remain controllable.

| Beat | Delivery |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Short, forceful, urgent |
| Stakes | Deliberate and controlled |
| Complexity | Faster, with clear consonants |
| Solution | Confident and grounded |
| Reasoning | Precise, with varied pitch |
| Uncertainty | Inquisitive; leave room for the question |
| Human control | Authoritative and reassuring |
| Outcome and CTA | Brighter, faster, optimistic |

Avoid robotic delivery by varying cues rather than applying one global setting. Do not make every line faster, louder, and higher. Use moderate compression and light vocal-presence enhancement.

Timing requirements:

- Begin narration within the first 100 milliseconds for an ad-like cut.
- Prevent accidental cue overlap or repeated phrases.
- Give each narrated clause a matching visual during the same interval.
- Leave short pauses only when the visual needs to land.

---

## 6. Build a visual plan

Use these visual families: trigger, operating context, evidence, product proof, reasoning, human review, outcome, and CTA.

For every clause, define what the viewer must literally see.

| Narration clause | Required meaning | Source | Source range | Edit range | Risk |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `{CLAUSE}` | `{VISUAL_MEANING}` | `{FILE}` | `{IN–OUT}` | `{IN–OUT}` | Brand, subtitle, mismatch, repetition |

Examples:

- “Compares prior incidents” needs history, retrieval, versions, or side-by-side comparison.
- “Conflicting signals” needs two differing representations or a visible mismatch.
- “Requests more evidence” needs a missing-input, confidence, or evidence-request state.
- “Approved action” needs human review before execution.

A visually impressive clip is wrong if it communicates a different idea from the narration.

### Diversity and pacing

- Use roughly 18–26 shots in a 45–60 second video.
- Keep most shots between 1.5 and 3 seconds.
- Do not reuse an exact shot accidentally.
- Minimize near-identical ranges from the same source.
- Use at least four visual families.
- Use product footage as proof, not as half of the video.
- Start with approximately 20–35% product footage and adjust for the audience.

### Exclusions

- Exclude talking heads unless explicitly approved.
- Exclude unrelated embedded subtitles.
- Replace clips with large unauthorized brands; crop only when composition remains strong.
- Review the complete range because logos, subtitles, or presenters may appear after the first frame.
- Reject empty loading states, long cursor movement, unreadable interfaces, and unrelated dashboards.

Record source provenance, rights status, useful ranges, visible brands, subtitles, presenters, resolution, and reuse count.

---

## 7. Use concise graphics

Create a small system from approved brand guidance: one background, one primary accent, warning and approval colors, two typefaces, one product frame, one logo safe area, and one transition language.

### Overlay rules

- One thought, 2–6 words, no more than two lines
- No duplicated phrase in adjacent shots or source footage
- Large text with a small container
- Minimal empty space inside boxes
- Consistent placement that avoids important imagery and platform controls
- Vertically centered end-card content

For 1920 × 1080, use approximately 72–120 px for impact text, 42–64 px for step labels, and 24–34 px for disclosures.

Use restrained motion: short accent rails, thin highlights, slow grids, controlled push-ins, and brief state pulses. Avoid decorative glitches or motion that implies behavior the product does not perform.

Prefer clean cuts. Use a match cut, audio bridge, or short controlled transition only when brightness, scale, motion, or subject position creates a jarring jump.

---

## 8. Add a real score

The score should move from tension at the trigger, through restrained investigation, toward brighter confidence and a resolved CTA.

Keep narration dominant through sidechain ducking or manual automation. Maintain music or intentional ambience at every frame, synchronize effects only with meaningful state changes, and end the score at the final frame.

Use short cues for detection, evidence requests, approval, execution, or CTA arrival—not for every cut.

---

## 9. Finish with an actionable end card

Use this formula:

> `{ACTION_VERB}` one `{HIGH_VALUE_WORKFLOW}` into a `{MEASURABLE_NEXT_STEP}`.

Include a small context label, one outcome-oriented headline, one line describing the next step, one button-style CTA, and an optional required disclosure.

Hold the card for at least 3.5 seconds. Vertically center the complete group and ensure any image is fully visible from its first frame.

---

## 10. Handle disclosure and distribution

| Status | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Official asset | Use approved branding, claims, review, and publishing channel |
| Approved concept | State that the workflow is representative if not deployed |
| Internal draft | Mark the file and distribution context as internal or draft |
| Personal concept | State in the title or description that it is not an official company video |

Generic public disclosure:

> This is a personal field-enablement concept. It is not an official `{PROVIDER_COMPANY}` or `{TARGET_COMPANY}` video, advertisement, case study, product announcement, or representation of a deployed workflow.

The disclosure does not need to dominate the thumbnail unless policy requires it.

### YouTube package

- Title: `{OUTCOME}: A {WORKFLOW_TYPE} Concept for {TARGET_COMPANY} | {PROVIDER_COMPANY}`
- Description: disclosure, problem, demonstrated workflow, synthetic-data statement, relevant link, focused hashtags
- Thumbnail: 1280 × 720, one focal image, one 2–5 word headline, large type, approved undistorted logos

### Master specification

- 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 30 fps
- H.264 High Profile, yuv420p
- AAC stereo, 48 kHz, approximately 192 kbps
- Fast-start metadata enabled
- Descriptive, versioned filename

Strip inherited source metadata and write the final title and artist deliberately.

Treat channel derivatives as separate edits. Reframe shots, rebuild overlays and end cards, and respect platform safe areas. Do not mechanically crop a horizontal master.

---

## 11. Production workflow

1. **Brief:** define viewer, workflow, decision, outcome, CTA, channel, and disclosure.
2. **Ingest:** collect approved footage; rename it; record provenance and useful ranges.
3. **Script:** write one short decision narrative and remove unsupported claims.
4. **Map:** assign one literal visual to every narration clause.
5. **Rough cut:** build the opening, product proof, human control, outcome, and CTA.
6. **Narration:** generate or record separate directed cues.
7. **Audio:** add an evolving score, duck it under narration, and remove gaps.
8. **Finish:** add concise overlays, correct crops, remove repetition, and smooth jarring cuts.
9. **Deliver:** write metadata, export the master, prepare the YouTube package, and document the edit map.

Use the smallest practical reproducible toolchain. Keep narration timing in structured data, overlays in a timed format, source ranges explicit, and generated exports separate from source footage.

---

## 12. Final checklist

### Story

- [ ] One viewer, workflow, decision, and CTA are clear.
- [ ] The trigger appears within three seconds.
- [ ] Claims distinguish concept, demonstration, deployment, and measured outcome.
- [ ] Human responsibility is explicit.

### Visuals

- [ ] Every narration clause has a matching visual.
- [ ] No prohibited presenters, subtitles, brands, or repeated shots remain.
- [ ] Product footage is concise and legible.
- [ ] The end-card image is complete from its first frame.

### Text and audio

- [ ] Overlays are short, large, minimally boxed, and non-duplicative.
- [ ] Narration is varied, intelligible, correctly pronounced, and non-overlapping.
- [ ] Music supports every frame and ducks beneath the voice.
- [ ] No unexpected silence or repeated narration remains.

### Delivery

- [ ] Logos, disclosure, claims, filename, and metadata are correct.
- [ ] Runtime and technical format match the channel.
- [ ] The master, thumbnail, script, build definition, and edit map are documented.
