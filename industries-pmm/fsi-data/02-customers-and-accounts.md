# FSI Customers, Priority Accounts, and Evidence Matrix

**Purpose:** Single source of truth for customer classification, repeatable proof, target-account priorities and unresolved evidence gaps.

---

## 1. Executive View

The public FSI evidence supports three repeatable GTM conclusions:

1. **Deployment choice matters more than one universal architecture.** Mistral appears across self-hosted, bank-infrastructure, private-cloud and internal multi-model environments.
2. **Financial document intelligence is the most repeatable workload pattern.** Ardian, HSBC and BNP Paribas provide the strongest public anchors.
3. **Multi-model coexistence is normal.** The strongest strategy is often to become a valuable model / deployment layer inside the customer's broader AI estate.

The evidence does **not** support broad claims about customer ARR, universal production scope, competitive win rates, or regulation as the causal purchase driver.

---

## 2. Evidence Classification

Every organization receives one primary classification:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Confirmed Production Mistral Customer** | Live Mistral use is explicitly supported |
| **2. Strategic Partnership — Production Early / Unclear** | Signed partnership / co-development; mature production scope not yet proven |
| **3. Confirmed Multi-Model User Including Mistral** | Mistral is one model inside a broader enterprise environment |
| **4. Confirmed Indirect / ISV-Embedded Usage** | Usage is through a third-party solution, not equivalent to direct procurement |
| **5. Investor / Ecosystem Relationship Only** | Relationship exists without confirmed software usage |
| **6. Target Account — No Confirmed Usage** | Commercially relevant prospect without verified deployment |
| **7. Adjacent Non-FSI Reference** | Useful proof excluded from FSI customer counts |
| **8. Unsupported / Requires Validation** | Evidence does not meet the bar for a stronger category |

### Attribution rules

- Relationship != investment != partnership != deployment != production.
- Marketplace / model availability != customer usage.
- ISV usage != direct Mistral procurement.
- Customer-wide AI metrics != Mistral-specific metrics unless explicitly tied.
- On-premises != air-gapped unless explicitly stated.
- Regulation / strategic autonomy may provide context; do not infer purchase causality.

## 6. Repeatable Evidence Patterns

### Pattern A — Deployment is a spectrum

- La Banque Postale: bank infrastructure / on-premises
- HSBC: self-hosted on HSBC systems
- Ardian: secure private-cloud / multi-model environment
- BNP Paribas: internal multi-model enterprise infrastructure

**GTM implication:** sell **deployment choice and control**, not “on-prem everywhere.”

### Pattern B — Multi-model architecture is normal

Strongest evidence: BNP Paribas, Ardian, AXA, BPCE.

**GTM implication:** “and” is often more realistic than “or.” Position Mistral as an indispensable model / workload layer inside the broader estate.

### Pattern C — Document intelligence is the most repeatable FSI workload

Strongest evidence: Ardian, HSBC, BNP Paribas.

**GTM implication:** lead with extraction, comparison, source-grounded analysis, lending / financing documents and due diligence before generic “AI for compliance.”

### Pattern D — Employee AI is a common adoption path

Evidence appears across BNP Paribas, HSBC, La Banque Postale, AXA and Ardian-style internal platforms.

**GTM implication:** use employee AI to land / expand, but keep it horizontal rather than the core FSI thesis.

### Pattern E — Co-development matters

BNP Paribas, La Banque Postale, ABN AMRO and HSBC all support close technical collaboration themes.

**GTM implication:** FDE / engineering collaboration can be part of the value proposition for complex deployments. Do not infer retention or revenue impact without internal data.

### Pattern F — Sovereignty is regional, not universal

Strongest public evidence is European.

**GTM implication:** globally lead with control, workload value and deployment choice; emphasize sovereignty / autonomy where the buyer explicitly values it.

## 8. Account Activation Readiness

Before activating an account, assess:

- strategic fit;
- priority-play / field-motion fit;
- named trigger;
- sponsor access;
- technical readiness;
- control / governance requirement;
- current AI stack / coexistence path;
- partner / ecosystem route;
- competitive entrenchment.

Classify each account:

- **Activate Now** — fit + trigger + plausible sponsor / route.
- **Develop** — strong fit but missing timing, sponsor or technical path.
- **Monitor** — attractive account without a current differentiated motion.

Avoid unexplained numerical account scores.

---

## 9. Public Evidence Gaps

Public research does not reliably establish:

- contract value / ARR / ACV by FSI customer;
- exact model versions deployed at each account;
- production-vs-pilot split for every announced use case;
- customer-specific ROI beyond published metrics;
- complete insurance workload coverage;
- regulator / central-bank customer status;
- Mistral-specific competitive win / loss rates;
- partner-generated pipeline or co-sell conversion;
- exact internal architecture when the customer has not published it;
- customer willingness to participate in public references.

Use these as **internal validation questions**, not assumptions.
