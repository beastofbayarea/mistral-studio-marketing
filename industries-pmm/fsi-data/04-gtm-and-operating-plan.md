# FSI GTM Strategy & Operating Plan (6–12 Months)

---

## 1. Executive GTM Thesis

Mistral should turn a small number of high-quality FSI proof points into **repeatable field motions**, not a broad collection of disconnected campaigns.

### Global thesis

> **Controlled AI for Financial Intelligence and Regulated Workflows**

This framing travels better than a global “sovereign AI” campaign:

- **Control** works across regions.
- **Financial intelligence** anchors the story in high-value workloads.
- **Regulated workflows** captures governance without claiming regulation forces one architecture.
- **Sovereignty / strategic autonomy** remains a strong regional theme where buyers explicitly value it.
- **Multi-model coexistence** reflects how major financial institutions actually adopt AI.

### Portfolio priorities

1. **P0 — Controlled AI for Regulated Financial Workflows**
2. **P0 — Financial Intelligence & Document Automation**
3. **P1 — Developer & IT AI expansion**
4. **Employee AI — horizontal land / expand motion, not the differentiated FSI thesis**

### Core field motions

- Financial Document Intelligence
- KYC / AML / financial-crime investigation support
- Due Diligence & Deal Analysis
- Research / Credit / Investment Intelligence
- Developer & IT AI expansion

### Operating principle

> **Prove -> Package -> Repeat -> Measure -> Prune / Scale**

Do not set revenue, win-rate or sales-cycle targets until internal baselines are validated.

| **ABN AMRO** | strategic autonomy / control / co-development; production maturity early / unclear |
| **Groupe Mutuel** | insurance / protection partnership; production maturity early / unclear |

### Lighthouse operating loop

```text
Confirmed proof
    -> validate exact claim
    -> quantify value / adoption where available
    -> confirm customer referenceability
    -> package proof for one field motion
    -> identify adjacent expansion
    -> reuse in analogous accounts
    -> capture objections / wins / losses / Product gaps
    -> refresh GTM and Product priorities
```

For each lighthouse maintain:

- confirmed workload;
- evidence strength;
- referenceability status;
- expansion hypothesis;
- analogous accounts;
- evidence still needed;
- next commercial / Product decision.

Do not assume a public logo is automatically available for a quantified case study or reference call.

## 4. Flagship Campaign

# **Your Data, Your Models, Your Rules**

### Campaign thesis

> **Control AI on the terms each financial workload requires.**

Financial institutions do not need one deployment pattern or one model provider for every use case. Mistral's role is to help them apply the right model and control model to high-value financial workflows.

### Primary buyers

- CIO / CDO / Head of AI
- CISO
- CRO / CCO
- Head of Credit / Underwriting
- Head of Investment / Research

### Message hierarchy

1. **Control where the workload runs** — self-hosted, private, managed or data-platform route.
2. **Control which model is used** — coexist with the broader model estate.
3. **Control how it is adapted and evaluated** — customer-specific data and golden sets where useful.
4. **Control access and evidence** — identity, policy, human review and traceability.
5. **Prove value on a financial workflow** — real documents, real decision criteria, no generic benchmark theater.

### Proof strategy

- HSBC / La Banque Postale — controlled deployment
- BNP Paribas — multi-model enterprise adoption / co-development
- Ardian — financial document intelligence and adoption
- AXA / BPCE — multi-model coexistence when exact source wording supports the claim
- ABN AMRO / Groupe Mutuel — strategic / regional-control proof, clearly labeled early / unclear

### Hero asset

**Controlled Financial AI Playbook**

Structure:

1. workload-control problem;
2. deployment-choice spectrum;
3. customer proof by field motion;
4. reference architectures with FDE validation labels;
5. customer-specific evaluation framework;
6. CTA to architecture + workload workshop.

### Evaluation CTA

Do not use a generic “Mistral vs competitor” benchmark as the centerpiece. Use customer-relevant data, explicit architecture, source traceability, customer-defined success criteria, known limitations and a clear next decision.

> **Run a 30–60 minute architecture and workload workshop, then agree one customer-specific evaluation.**

For named strategic accounts, prioritize direct account engagement over gated-content lead capture.

## 7. Competitive Routing

Classify each opportunity:

- **WIN** — evidence-backed requirement maps directly to Mistral advantage.
- **WEDGE** — Mistral owns one strategic workload inside an incumbent architecture.
- **COEXIST** — customer retains hyperscaler / internal platform; Mistral adds a model / deployment / customization path.
- **PARTNER** — vertical application or GSI owns workflow / implementation; Mistral supplies model-layer value.
- **AVOID FORCED COMPETITION** — no differentiated workload, or the incumbent owns the application layer.

### Do not force competition when

- replacing generic M365 / Workspace productivity;
- replacing specialized KYC / sanctions / claims systems simply because they use AI;
- building a full retail-banking application when model selection is only one component;
- replacing a mature internal AI platform at a sophisticated bank.

The preferred posture is often **coexistence or workload wedge**, not wholesale displacement.

## 8. Five Deal-Acceleration Mechanisms

| Blocker | Mechanism | Primary output | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use-case ambiguity | **Workload Prioritization Worksheet** | ranked workloads | production-relevant evaluation selected |
| Security / architecture uncertainty | **Controlled Deployment & Security Blueprint** | architecture, data flow, responsibilities, open questions | architecture / security decision progresses |
| Accuracy / trust uncertainty | **Workload Evaluation Kit** | golden-set results with source traceability | explicit evaluation decision |
| POV scope creep | **POV Decision Blueprint** | scope, dataset, success criteria, owners, decision date | next-stage decision reached |
| Procurement / economics uncertainty | **Deployment Economics Model** | scenario economics using customer inputs | commercial decision progresses |

Use **Financial AI Controls Mapping** only as a requirement-to-control aid; it is not a compliance certification.

Downloads and asset views are not proof of effectiveness. Measure whether the mechanism changes the next decision.

4. validated proof;
5. competitive routing;
6. FDE-approved evaluation path;

8. CTA / next decision;

### Enablement modules

1. What each customer logo actually proves
2. P0 play / field-motion discovery
3. Win / wedge / coexist / partner / avoid routing
4. Current capability vs Product-gap discipline
5. Use of the five deal accelerators

A formal certification is optional; repeatable field execution is the objective.

## 12. Decision-Oriented Cadence

### Weekly / frequent field triage

**Inputs:** active blockers, objections, proof gaps, new customer evidence  
**Decision:** asset update, escalation, Product issue, proof requirement, or no action

### Monthly FSI play + pipeline review

**Inputs:** pipeline by play / motion, target-account progression, competitive outcomes, deal-accelerator use, partner contribution  
**Decision:** reallocate focus, improve qualification, change / remove a motion, or escalate a repeated blocker

### Monthly / quarterly win-loss review

**Decision:** messaging, qualification, competitive, Product or partner action

### Quarterly VoC / Product review

**Decision:** already supported, integration pattern, FDE pattern, Product gap, partner opportunity, or deprioritize

Every recurring forum should produce a decision, not a status report.

## 13. 30 / 60 / 90 / 180-Day Sequence

### Days 1–30 — Learn and validate

- reconcile public customer evidence with internal account truth;
- audit active FSI opportunities and repeated blockers;
- align Sales / FDE / Product on the two P0 plays;
- validate Product capability / gap assumptions;
- validate lighthouse referenceability;
- select priority accounts using activation readiness;
- agree baseline reporting with RevOps;
- audit existing assets before creating new ones.

### Days 31–60 — Enable and prove

- publish concise playbook / proof library;
- operationalize the five deal accelerators;
- create the first validated lighthouse proof asset;
- run customer-specific evaluations with explicit production decisions;
- establish win/loss + objection coding;
- test the flagship campaign message with a small priority-account cohort.

### Days 61–90 — Activate selectively

- launch the highest-confidence account / campaign motion;
- deepen lighthouse expansion;
- activate only partners with verified relationship, technical relevance and account access;
- measure evaluation and field-motion progression;
- feed repeated blockers into Product / FDE review.

### Days 91–180 — Scale what works

- compare play / field-motion performance;
- prune low-performing assets, campaigns and partner motions;
- scale account patterns that repeatedly progress;
- expand partner involvement only where it demonstrably changes opportunities;
- refresh Product priorities from accumulated evidence;
- set the next six-month targets from internal baselines and observed conversion.
